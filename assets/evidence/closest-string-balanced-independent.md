The choice blocks give a clause for each illegal individual name or inadmissible pair of names in different blocks. Thus the satisfying choices are precisely the ordered selections of a clique. The two fresh fixed bits preserve satisfiability. Each constructed clause refers to a bit position at most once, so its partial word is well-defined. On the nontrivial source instances the word length is at least four. Every added symbol occurs in a completion of a unit clause, and both bit values occur in specified positions. This proves the promised equality for the number of actually occurring symbols.

A satisfying assignment hits a specified bit of every clause. Conversely, even a center that uses a different additional symbol in every position still omits at least one of the 2L−1 additional symbols. For every clause, the word completed entirely with that omitted symbol forces agreement at a specified bit. Changing the other positions to zero leaves each witnessed agreement intact. This establishes correctness for unrestricted centers on the promised instances.

There are polynomially many explicit words of polynomial length with polynomial-size binary names, since k ≤ n and the input represents its vertices explicitly. With d = k⌈log₂ n⌉+1, the threshold T(2k) gives h(d) ≤ log₂ n+1 above the threshold. Below it, the maximum of the computable envelope over the finite range is computable from k. The fixed polynomial input factor therefore has degree independent of k, and the resulting deterministic Clique algorithm is FPT in k. This checks the exclusion with effective convergence, a computable upper envelope, and no alphabet-dependent prefactor. The imported Clique completeness statement is Corollary 3.2 of Downey and Fellows. [Author copy, p. 14](https://homepages.ecs.vuw.ac.nz/~downey/publications/2.pdf#page=14).
