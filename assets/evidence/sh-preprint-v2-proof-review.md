# Independent review of SH proof revision 2

On 19 November 2026, Codex agent originals independently reviewed the four Atlas manuscripts after the updates to the SH citation and to the attribution of the Vertex Cover kernel. The review checks the cited decision interfaces and their use in the manuscripts. The sparsification lemma itself is an external import.

Lemma 2.2 of v2 gives a deterministic decision disjunction at fixed width three. For any fixed $0<d<1$, there are at most $2^{O(v^{1-d})}$ branches on the original variables, with $O(v^{1+Cd})$ clauses each for a width-dependent constant $C$. Generation has the same exponential factor and a fixed polynomial input factor. Relabeling gives at most a logarithmic factor on the branch encoding length. For any hypothetical CNF exponent $b<1$, choose $d$ sufficiently small that $b(1+Cd)<1$. A fixed exponent strictly between $\max\{1-d,b(1+Cd)\}$ and one absorbs the logarithm and the sum of generation and evaluation exponents. This supplies the decision interface for Clique, the three classical graph bounds, and 3-Coloring. [MIT Hardness Group, v2, Lemma 2.2, pp. 6–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02650v2#page=6)

The selection-bit encodings for Clique, Independent Set, and Vertex Cover impose the respective pair constraints and use a linear-size balanced addition circuit for the threshold. The Vertex Cover budget statement first applies the Nemhauser–Trotter decomposition in the kernelization by Chen, Kanj, and Jia. Their journal Theorem 2.2 gives at most $2k_2$ vertices for a reduced budget $k_2$ at most $k$, after any immediate decisions. The use of exact-size covers in the source is equivalent to the threshold convention after checking oversized thresholds. The 3-Coloring encoding uses exactly three variables per explicit vertex and width-at-most-three color constraints. These constructions preserve the required parameter in the exponent with polynomial complete-input overhead. [Chen, Kanj, and Jia, Theorem 2.2, p. 284](https://doi.org/10.1006/jagm.2001.1186)

Lemma 2.1 of v2 supplies the decision lower bound by the sum of variables and clauses for width three. The Subset Sum proof makes two assignment items per variable and two slack items per clause, with maximum digit sum five in base eight. It selects exactly one assignment item per variable, and the slack digits reach the target precisely for satisfied clauses. The item count is linear and the total explicit binary encoding length is polynomial. [MIT Hardness Group, v2, Lemma 2.1, pp. 5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02650v2#page=5)
