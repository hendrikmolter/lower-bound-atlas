The proof starts with failure of SH at one power $b<1$. I checked the width-three quantitative sparsification interface in the cited version. For a fixed sufficiently small $d>0$, generation has exponent $1-d<1$, and each formula's relabeled encoding gives exponent at most $b(1+Dd)<1$ before a logarithmic factor. Choosing one strict intermediate power $a<1$ absorbs that logarithm and bounds the product of enumeration and per-formula running times. This yields one deterministic width-three SAT algorithm with exponent $O(v^a)$ in the variable count $v$ and a fixed polynomial in the explicit input length. The quantitative sparsification lemma is a preprint dependency. [MIT Hardness Group, arXiv v1, Lemma 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02650v1)

I checked the width-three encoding. The edge clauses implement the two respective graph properties, and the threshold circuit uses $O(n)$ fanin-two gates since there are $O(n/2^j)$ additions of $O(j)$ bits at each level $j$. Gate constraints enforce a consistent evaluation and the final unit clause enforces the comparison. Thus the number of formula variables is $O(n+1)$, even for dense graphs, and its complete length is polynomial in $B$. The budget route first applies the published $2k$-vertex kernel and feeds the resulting graph and updated threshold to the same encoding. The bounded and out-of-range thresholds are handled in polynomial time. Composing with the derived SAT solver violates each respective exclusion separately. [Nemhauser and Trotter](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01580444) · [Jansen and Bodlaender, kernel statement](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-012-9393-4)
