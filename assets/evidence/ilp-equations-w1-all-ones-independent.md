The incidence test uses the endpoint in the row's color class, as given in Lin's Lemma 14 (ICALP 2019, pp. 81:11–81:12). For any pair of colors, a constant-function row sums all and only the variables for edges of that pair. Every cross-color column appears in such a row. Nonnegative integer solutions to these rows choose exactly one edge per pair and every coordinate is binary. Two chosen edges at one color that use distinct vertices differ at some bit position. The function sending the two bit values to the indices of their respective partner colors gives a row containing both edges and violates the right-hand side of one. This proves endpoint agreement in every color. The edges of any multicolored clique satisfy all rows because the function evaluated at the common vertex's bit selects exactly one partner color. Trivial source inputs can be decided before any solver invocation.

The exact row count ℓ(ℓ−1)²⌈log₂ n⌉ is at most 2ℓ³ log₂ n on the remaining branch and the column count is at most n². The complete encoding length and reduction time have bounds with a constant power of n. With j = 2ℓ³, the effective tail gives h(k) ≤ log₂ n for k ≥ T(j). Fix one finite integer H bounding the values at indices below T(1). The remaining indices below T(j) have h(k) ≤ k and hence are bounded by T(j). The algorithm and its computable FPT time bound need only the fixed integer H and the computable threshold, so pointwise computability of h is unnecessary.

The all-ones, nonzero-column restriction gives a subset dynamic program in 2^k N^{O(1)} time. A feasible vector is exactly a collection of columns with disjoint supports covering all rows, and the recurrence extends an attainable row subset by a disjoint support. The all-ones construction therefore yields an effective logarithmic-scale recovery against the general binary-coefficient ETH target at the k log₂ k scale. The proof supplies no O(k log₂ k) column cap. For example, three color classes of equal size in a complete tripartite graph give a logarithmic number of rows in n and a quadratic number of columns. The separate column-capped ETH target receives no recovery from this argument. Knop, Pilipczuk, and Wrochna's Theorem 1 was checked in the STACS edition.

The cited comparisons with Dvořák et al. Theorem 25 and Rohwedder and Węgrzycki Lemmas 15–16 correctly describe related covering, cardinality and complementary incidence formulations. They serve as context for the exact-one argument. This review establishes correctness and scope, and does not establish priority.
