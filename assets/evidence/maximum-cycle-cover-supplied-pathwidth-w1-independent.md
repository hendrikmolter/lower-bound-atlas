# Independent agent review of the Maximum Cycle Cover manuscript

Reviewer: Codex agent `/root/population_c`. Date: 18 November 2026.

Verdict: verified for the theorem excluding deterministic time $2^{h(p)}B^{O(1)}$ under FPT ≠ W[1], with $p$ the actual width of the supplied decomposition and $h$ nonnegative and finite with a computable modulus for $o(p\log p)$. The hard graphs are finite, simple, connected, bipartite and have maximum degree eight. The binary cover threshold and supplied decomposition are both part of $B$.

- The grouped Clique formula correctly bans invalid codes, repeated vertices and nonedges. A clause is hit if a selected group assignment makes one of its literals true. With half-logarithmic group size, the square order is $O(m/\log m)$. The constructive coloring family of LMS, Lemma 2.8, separates the chosen cells across rows and separates each chosen cell from all other cells in its own row. This gives a distinct-color embedding and preserves a unique preimage for every retained row-color pair. The extra row absorbs the single unused color. I checked the coloring property and range in the author version of the journal paper.
- For the sparse selector, any $d-1$ ports can be matched to its $d-1$ private vertices by switching the matching at the one missing port. First replacing cycles through private vertices costs at most the number of private vertices and free ports. Then freeing all but one port costs at most the number of newly installable private cycles. The single-center gadget similarly allows a nondecreasing replacement. Disjoint port sets protect normalized private cycles.
- The host soundness count forces precisely two distinguished vertices on every remaining cycle. An initial selector joins a row start to a distinct column start. An uncovered stage triangle vertex forces its corresponding first distinguished vertex onto an incoming cycle that closes across the two matching rails. Capacity one disallows another first cross-path in the same stage. All remaining occurrence triangles stay local, while the second cross-path for the broken triangle opens the next paired path. This checks the induction even with multiple permissible cells in a row. The bag sweep keeps each selector and occurrence adjacency covered with $O(K)$ live vertices.
- Subdivision of multigraph edges gives an exact packing correspondence. In the degree-reduction gadget the remaining occupied ports occur in pairs, and a deleted cycle using just one center must occupy a port. Hence the center charge pays for deleting extra visiting cycles so that the triangles fit. The backbone decomposition has constant overhead per live original vertex.
- The directed edge gadgets pay one private cycle per old edge, leaving at most one available direction for each edge on projection. The directed spanning wrapper has exactly two configurations at a vertex, using a private cycle either alone or through the old vertex. Forcing each internal path in the final bipartite cover corresponds exactly to one incoming and one outgoing selected arc. The graph stays connected and the maximum-degree count is $6+1+1=8$. Each conversion has constant multiplicative or additive width overhead and output of fixed polynomial length.
- With $\phi(p)=\max(1,p\log_2(p+2))=O(m)$, the computable modulus bounds the solver exponent above the cutoff. A fixed integer bounds the finite prefix at accuracy one. Monotonicity of $\phi$ then bounds the entire remaining prefix at the cutoff chosen as a computable function of Clique size, even if individual values of $h$ are not computable. The explicit OR overhead has the same type of computable envelope. The full algorithm for Clique has a fixed polynomial degree in graph order and a computable factor in Clique size.
