# Clique literature and equivalence checks

The literature baselines concern deterministic decision on explicit finite simple graphs. Vertex identifiers can be normalized in polynomial time. After immediate decisions for out-of-range Clique thresholds, canonical encodings of the graph, threshold, and any supplied partition have length polynomial in the graph order. Polynomial encoding factors are absorbed into the displayed data-dependent time bounds where appropriate.

## Clique by number of vertices

For Uniform ETH, the forward direction uses deterministic sparsification and the clause-count reduction in Theorem 5. A clause of width at most three creates at most three literal vertices. Compatibility between distinct clauses gives a clique with one choice per clause if and only if the formula is satisfiable. After sparsification with any fixed tolerance $1/j$, the graph has order $O_j(v)$ for $v$ SAT variables, so a single subexponential graph algorithm gives a solver in $2^{v/j+o(v)}$ up to polynomial factors. Run these correct solvers concurrently by a fixed-share schedule across all positive integers $j$ and return the first answer. Each fixed $j$ incurs at most a constant simulation share and polynomial or polylogarithmic overhead, which is subexponential after polynomial-time normalization of the formula to distinct width-three clauses on consecutively named variables. Equivalently, an efficient universal interpreter with a bounded polynomial overhead scales $1/j$ by at most a fixed constant. Thus there is one subexponential 3-SAT algorithm. The converse is obtained by the linear-variable encoding in Theorem 5. It uses one selection bit per vertex, clauses forbidding simultaneous selection of the ends of a nonedge, and a linear-size threshold circuit encoded with one variable per gate. The formula has $O(n)$ variables and polynomial complete length. These arguments match the uniform little-o convention. [Impagliazzo, Paturi and Zane, Corollary 2 and Theorem 5](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2001.1774)

The forward SH direction uses the published preprint statement for Independent Set by graph order. Graph complementation preserves the threshold and vertex count with polynomial full-input overhead. The complete fixed-power family is required for the converse, which uses quantitative sparsification for width three in the version-specific preprint dependency. [MIT Hardness Group et al., v2, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02650v2#page=4) · [Impagliazzo, Paturi and Zane, Theorem 5](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2001.1774) · [Independent converse review](clique-order-sh-converse-independent.md)

## Clique by solution size and Multicolored Clique by number of color classes

For the ordinary FPT exclusions, the forward direction for Clique is Downey and Fellows's W[1]-hardness, and the converse is W[1]-membership from the same completeness theorem. Exact-size and threshold Clique coincide by taking a subset. For Multicolored Clique, the forward direction uses $k$ copies of the vertices of a Clique instance, one in each class. Add edges between copies in different classes exactly when their original vertices were adjacent. This preserves the parameter and is polynomial after oversized Clique thresholds have been decided. Conversely, delete edges within classes and use the same graph with threshold $k$ as a Clique instance. A $k$-clique then uses all classes. This shows membership in W[1] and hence proves the converse to the ordinary no-FPT exclusion. Equivalent input clocks follow from polynomial encoding normalization. [Downey and Fellows, Corollary 3.2](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(94)00097-3) · [Fellows, Hermelin, Rosamond and Vialette, §2, Lemma 1 and the following observation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2008.09.065)

The same two maps preserve the unrestricted $f(k)n^{o(k)}$ benchmark between the tasks. In the forward reduction the $k$ multiplier in the graph order can be absorbed into a computable prefactor because an $o(k)$ function is bounded above by $Ak$ for a suitable constant $A$. This transfers the Szeider lower bound to Multicolored Clique and recovers the historical ETH exclusion under the square hypothesis in both cases. The same reductions do not supply a converse from the budget-based exclusion to ETH or the square hypothesis. [Szeider, Proposition 1 and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24088151) · [Fellows, Hermelin, Rosamond and Vialette, §2, Lemma 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2008.09.065) · [Lokshtanov, Marx and Saurabh, Theorem 5.2](https://bulletin.eatcs.org/index.php/beatcs/article/view/92)

The SH proofs use a prefactor bounded by an exponential of a fixed polynomial in $k$ and a fixed power saving in the graph-order exponent. The source clause-grouping graph itself is multicolored, since every class is independent. These families preserve both restrictions in the clock and leave the unrestricted numerical converse unassessed. The older recognizable clause-grouping image equivalences need their own input language and exact prefactor conventions. Ordinary no-FPT excludes only constant graph-order exponents. Neither of these two alternatives supplies a close recovery of the historical unrestricted little-o benchmark. [Koutecký, Theorem 3 and its clause-grouping proof](https://iuuk.mff.cuni.cz/~koutecky/paralg2627-graph/lecture-2-proving-the-hard-side.html)
