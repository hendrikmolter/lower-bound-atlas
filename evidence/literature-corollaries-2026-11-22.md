## Closest String

Szeider's Table 1 first row transfers the unrestricted Closest String bounds under the square-clique premise. The exact balanced promise follows from the construction of Lokshtanov, Marx, and Saurabh. Corollary 2.6 and Lemma 2.8 give an explicit deterministic disjunction of `2^{O(k log log  k)}` square permutation instances. The constructions in Theorems 2.11 and 2.12 use two diagonal blocks and include one set for each row confined to its block. In Theorem 3.1 the strings have length `h=2k`, radius `h-1`, `h` row symbols and `h+1` dummy symbols. For any row set, its own row symbol appears within its block and each dummy symbol appears outside that block in its corresponding string. Hence all `2h+1` symbols occur, and each query has polynomial size in `k`. A claimed solver would take `2^{o(k log k)}` over the entire disjunction. Szeider's result was located through the Atlas audit of the supplied October 30 version. [Published reduction](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1104834) · [Open proof text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.08723) · [Szeider preprint](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23797197)

## Edge Clique Cover

Cygan, Pilipczuk, and Pilipczuk explicitly state the lower bound in Corollary 3. In the converse direction, Gramm et al.'s Theorem 1 and Rules 1 and 3′ give a reduced graph on at most `2^k` vertices. For ordinary graph instances one can remove isolated vertices, remove a single-edge component while decrementing the budget, and remove one of two adjacent true twins outside such a component. In the last case a clique covering a surviving edge at the remaining twin can also cover the removed pair. A reduced yes-instance has at most one vertex per nonempty clique-membership signature. Compare a binary budget with the edge count at the start, accepting immediately when every edge can be covered separately. Encoding the reduced graph by its adjacency matrix and its budget in binary takes `2^{O(k+1)}` bits. A cover needs at most one chosen covering clique per edge as a certificate. Thus `NP ⊆ QP` gives a deterministic algorithm within the stated parameter dependence. [Published lower bound](https://doi.org/10.1137/130947076) · [Open Corollary 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.1754v2) · [Kernel proof](https://fpt.akt.tu-berlin.de/homepage-hueffner/clique-cover-jea07.pdf)

## Membership directions

- **Grid Tiling.** Definition II.4 and Remark II.5 in Curticapean and Xia include the full `k × k` array. Theorem II.6 states W[1]-completeness for the decision variant. Interchanging the coordinate names reconciles the two conventions. The book's Theorem 14.28 supplies the hardness construction already cited by the Atlas. [FOCS proceedings](https://ieee-focs.org/FOCS-2015-Papers/8191a994.pdf) · [Parameterized Algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-21275-3)
- **Scattered Set and planar Scattered Set.** The graph-power characterization in Hoang's section on graphs and their powers gives a reduction to Independent Set without changing the requested size. Breadth-first search computes the pairwise comparisons for unweighted graphs. Vertices in different components have infinite distance. At threshold one, every pair of distinct vertices qualifies and the answer is determined by the vertex count. The resulting graph is an unrestricted Independent Set instance. For hardness in the general case, distance two gives Independent Set. For planar inputs, the reduction in the book's Theorem 14.32 uses an input threshold and a requested size depending only on the source grid side. [Graph-power characterization](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2024.114682) · [Parameterized Algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-21275-3)
- **Fixed-width exact-weight CNF.** For fixed width, each clause is a constraint over a finite family of Boolean relations. Marx's dichotomy puts weighted satisfiability for that family in W[1], including the FPT alternative. Tautologies on any explicitly listed unused variables retain their eligibility for the exact weight. The existing lower-bound sources use fixed width and exact weight. [Boolean constraint dichotomy](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00037-005-0195-9)
- **Unit disks and axis-parallel unit squares.** The membership arguments construct the intersection graph in polynomial bit time using rational coordinate comparisons and preserve the selection budget. For hardness, Marx's two constructions use open regions. Van Leeuwen's Theorems 4.1.8 and 4.1.10 give the size-preserving transfer to closed regions via common dilation. For an explicit bit implementation, let `r<1` be the largest ratio of an intersecting pair, or zero if none intersect. For disks use the squared distance over the squared diameter. For squares use the maximum coordinate difference over side length. Multiply the centers by `(3-r)/2>1` while keeping the regions' sizes. The disk ratio multiplier is the square of this factor, and the square ratio multiplier is the factor itself. The largest intersecting ratio remains below one since `r(3-r)^2/4-1=(r-1)^2(r-4)/4<0`. All other ratios become strictly greater than one. The operation preserves the budget, the number of objects, and polynomial bit length. If the source uses diameter one and the target uses radius one, also double the centers and the radii. The same diameter convention in the lower-bound reductions does not change the parameter or exponent. [Marx, Theorems 1 and 3](https://www.cs.bme.hu/~dmarx/papers/marx-esa2005.pdf) · [Van Leeuwen, Chapters 4.1 and 4.3](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/1075216/144795_thesis.pdf)
