Problems / Cluster Editing

Subexponential bound in the edit budget under ETH

Statement

Input restrictions
  • The input graph has maximum degree at most six.

No deterministic algorithm solves Cluster Editing within the stated running time.

2o(k)⁢n𝒪(1)2^(o(k)) n^(O(1))

  • k — maximum number of edge additions and deletions
  • n — number of vertices in the input graph

ETH ⇒ Statement

  • Cluster Editing with Locally Bounded Modifications · Christian Komusiewicz, Johannes Uhlmann · Discrete Applied Mathematics 160(15) (2012), pp. 2259–2270 · Theorem 2 and proof, §2, pp. 2263–2264 · Open full text

Equivalence to Uniform ETH

Uniform ETH ⇒ Statement

  • Christian Komusiewicz and Johannes Uhlmann, Cluster Editing with Locally Bounded Modifications, Discrete Applied Mathematics 160 (2012), pp. 2259–2270. Section 2, Theorems 1 and 2 and their 3-SAT reduction.
  • Russell Impagliazzo, Ramamohan Paturi and Francis Zane, Which Problems Have Strongly Exponential Complexity?, Journal of Computer and System Sciences 63 (2001), pp. 512–530. Corollary 1 and its proof, fixed-width decision sparsification.
Derivation from the cited sources

IPZ sparsification at rate 1/j1/j gives at most 2v/j2^(v/j) formulas with Oj(v)O_j(v) clauses each. The degree-six reduction gives each a budget Oj(v)O_j(v). Interleaving these algorithms over j turns a subexponential Cluster Editing solver into one subexponential 3-SAT solver.

Statement ⇒ Uniform ETH

  • Alexander Firbas and Manuel Sorge, Cluster Editing in Subexponential Fixed-Parameter Time, arXiv:2611.27956v1, Theorem 1.3 and its proof in Section 5.2.
Derivation from the cited sources

An m-clause 3-SAT formula has at most 3m occurring variables. If uniform ETH fails, the transfer therefore takes 2𝒪(k⁢log(r)/r)+o(k)2^(O(k log(r)/r)+o(k)) times a polynomial in the graph size for each fixed r. Run the transfer for all admissible r in parallel. Every fixed r is eventually admissible, and log(r)/rlog(r)/r tends to zero, giving one subexponential algorithm in k, including on graphs of maximum degree six.
