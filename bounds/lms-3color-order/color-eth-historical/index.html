Problems / 3-Coloring

Subexponential-time bound under ETH

Statement

Problem domain
  • The graph is finite, simple, undirected, and unweighted.

No deterministic algorithm decides 3-Coloring within the stated running time.

2o(n)⁢B𝒪(1)2^(o(n)) B^(O(1))

  • n — number of vertices
  • B — input length in bits

ETH ⇒ Statement

  • Which Problems Have Strongly Exponential Complexity? · Russell Impagliazzo, Ramamohan Paturi, Francis Zane · JCSS 63(4) (2001), 512–530 · Theorem 4, p. 525 · Open full text
  • Lower bounds based on the Exponential Time Hypothesis · Daniel Lokshtanov, Dániel Marx, Saket Saurabh · Bulletin of the EATCS 105 (2011), 41–71 · Theorem 3.2, PDF p. 12 · Open full text

Equivalence to Uniform ETH

Uniform ETH ⇒ Statement

  • Daniel Lokshtanov, Dániel Marx, and Saket Saurabh. “Lower bounds based on the Exponential Time Hypothesis.” Bulletin of the EATCS 105, 41–71 (2011). Section 2 (the subexponential-time formulation for 3-CNF-SAT) and Theorem 3.2 (3-Coloring on unrestricted graphs).
  • George B. Mertzios and Paul G. Spirakis. “Algorithms and Almost Tight Results for 3-Colorability of Small Diameter Graphs.” Algorithmica 74, 385–414 (2016). Section 2, definition of ETH and Theorem 2 (diameter 4 and radius 2).

Statement ⇒ Uniform ETH

  • Daniel Faber, Adalat Jabrayilov, and Petra Mutzel. “SAT Encoding of Partial Ordering Models for Graph Coloring Problems.” 27th International Conference on Theory and Applications of Satisfiability Testing (SAT 2024), LIPIcs 305, article 12 (2024). Assignment clauses (8a) and (8b), specialized here to three colors.

Historical status

The 2026 algorithm for 3-SAT refutes strongly uniform ETH. Encoding each vertex with three variables therefore gives a 3-Coloring algorithm whose exponent is effectively sublinear in the number of vertices.
