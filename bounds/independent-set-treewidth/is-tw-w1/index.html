Effective little-o bound in the treewidth under FPT ≠ W[1]

Statement

Problem domain
  • The graph is finite, simple, undirected, and unweighted.

No deterministic algorithm decides Independent Set within the stated running time.

2oeff(t)⁢B𝒪(1)2^(o_eff(t)) B^(O(1)) Effective little-o

  • t — treewidth of the input graph
  • B — complete input length in bits

FPT ≠ W[1] ⇒ Statement

  • XNLP-Completeness for Parameterized Problems on Graphs with a Linear Structure · Hans L. Bodlaender, Carla Groenland, Hugo Jacob, Lars Jaffke, Paloma T. Lima · Algorithmica 87 (2025), 465–506 · Theorem 6 (logarithmic pathwidth).
  • On the Complexity of Problems on Tree-Structured Graphs · Hans L. Bodlaender, Carla Groenland, Hugo Jacob, Marcin Pilipczuk, Michał Pilipczuk · IPEC 2022, LIPIcs 249, 6:1–6:17 · Theorem 11 (logarithmic treewidth).
Derivation from the cited sources

Theorem 6 gives W[1]-hardness through XNLP-hardness for pathwidth divided by the logarithm of the graph order. For a Clique instance of length M and parameter k, the reduction therefore produces a graph of pathwidth at most g(k)⁢(1+log2⁢M)g(k)(1+log₂ M) for a computable g. Treewidth satisfies the same upper bound. Using the effective cutoff with accuracy 1/g(k)1/g(k) bounds the exponential factor by a computable function of k times M. The resulting Clique algorithm would be fixed-parameter tractable. Theorem 11 of the IPEC paper gives the width bound directly for Independent Set; the solver can ignore the supplied tree decomposition.

Recovery of a historical ETH bound

Effectively recovered · ETH target: Exponential in the treewidth

The exclusion reaches the historical treewidth exponent scale for effectively sublinear exponents.
